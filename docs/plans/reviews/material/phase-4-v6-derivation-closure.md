## A. The draft clause, corrected and ready to transcribe

**2.2.2B — a supersession must be TOTAL, and a partial one is defective.**

A clause that supersedes a rule of version 5 or version 4 REPLACES it entirely: after supersession the older rule is not in force at any wording, and no clause of this document may re-import it by reference. A superseding clause must therefore state, in this document's own words, everything the superseded rule stated — not only the part version 6 changes. A clause that states only what it adds has left the untouched part bound by nothing, which is the failure C6-D1 names: "superseding a total invariant and replacing it with a partial one leaves the six OLD members bound by nothing" (§4.1), and "a rule cannot survive the loss of the enumeration it quantified over" (§4.1). Such a clause is DEFECTIVE and a document relying on it does not conform.

**Two clauses of this document are already defective under this rule and are repaired with it, named here so the rule does not arrive with silent exceptions.** C6-FKM re-imports "the per-role key sets version 4 gives its ten roles, unchanged" (§7A) after §7A has declared the matrix that gathers them replaced. C6-S13 says "C5-S13's paragraph about which keys a searching loader must not read as names stands entire and unrepeated here" (§4.3) after 2.2.2A has superseded C5-S13's list. Each must either restate what it points at or the 2.2.2A row must be narrowed to name what it does not carry.

Four obligations bind every row of the 2.2.2A table. Each is decidable by a reader holding this document, version 5, version 4 and the shipped source, and none of them asks what a sentence means.

**T1 — every SITE is cited.** The row's "where it lives" cell names every place in version 5 and version 4 that states the superseded rule or lists its members, not merely the first. Where an enumeration is written twice — a defining section, a universal-key table and an appendix — all are cited. Version 5 met this obligation for the appendix case and its wording is the model: "This supersedes the corresponding table of version 4 section 14" (version 5, §14.2).

**T2 — every MEMBER is written out.** Where the superseded rule is a closed enumeration, the clauses named in "replaced by" write out, at their exact wire spellings and in code fences, every member of the version 6 enumeration: the carried members and the added members together, in one list, in one place. A count with no list does not satisfy this, and neither does a pointer of the form "version 5's ten" or "the six version 4 fixed": a pointer is a reference to a rule this document has just declared not in force. Where the enumeration has a shipped constant, the written list equals that constant plus exactly the members this document names as added; a difference wider than the named additions is a defect, and §2.1.2 decides which of the two is the defective one, because the shipped constants are version 5's and this document is written before any version 6 code exists.

**T3 — the SUCCESSOR is itself total.** Every clause named in a "replaced by" cell states the whole of the superseded rule. A cell may name more than one clause, and a cell may name §14; totality is judged over the named set. A clause that is total only when read together with a clause the cell does not name does not satisfy this: the cell is amended to name it.

**T4 — nothing is superseded off the table.** A clause of this document supersedes only what a row of 2.2.2A carries. A clause that names a further rule as superseded, that widens a rule quantified over a smaller set, or that changes an inherited fact's disposition, either has a row of its own or is defective. C6-K4 states the standard for this case in its own words: "adding a third list to a rule that quantified over two is exactly the case section 2.2.2 says must be a named supersession rather than a widening in place" (§5.4).

**What a reviewer confirms, mechanically.** For each row of 2.2.2A: (a) every clause identifier in "replaced by" exists as a clause opener in this document; (b) the distinct wire spellings written out in those clauses number exactly what this document says the enumeration holds, and equal the shipped constant plus the named additions; (c) no other line of version 5 or version 4 lists half or more of that enumeration's members, or states its size, without being cited in "where it lives"; (d) every invariant of version 5 or version 4 whose statement mentions the superseded rule's key name, or quantifies over the enumeration, is either restated here or listed, with its reason, in the carried-unchanged register the battery holds; (e) in the reverse direction, every supersession any clause of this document claims appears as a row. The battery is `tests/test_p4x6_supersession_totality.py` and a row failing any of the five is red.

**Why totality rather than a diff.** A diff is cheaper to write and cannot be checked: it is a statement about two documents rather than about a description, so no loader and no test holding one document can decide it. A total restatement is decidable by counting, which is why this clause is stated as a count and not as a standard of care.
