### 6.12 `affixed_number`

A column whose cells are a shared prefix, a number, and a shared
suffix: `$1,250`, `45%`, `5 mg`, `170cm`. The one shipped number
classifier reads group separators, a leading plus and accounting
parentheses, and it is the AFFIX that defeats it, so a column of
these fell to `free_text` unless an earlier rule claimed it, and
published nothing at all. This role reads the number out of the
cell, describes it with the quantitative apparatus of section 6.7,
and publishes the two affix spellings so a twin can write them back
onto its cells.

It is rule 10 of the order in section 5.2, tested after
`time_of_day` and before `long_tail_labels`.

#### When a column takes this role

**C6-4 (what it is).** A column takes this role when every earlier
rule of section 5.2 has declined it, and at least the parse-line
count of its present cells are affixed numbers wearing ONE affix
pair whose cell count is at least `small_cell_floor`. A cell is an
affixed number when its trimmed text is `prefix + core + suffix`
where the core is a substring the one shipped number classifier —
the same classifier every other role reads cells with, with its
acceptance of group separators, a leading plus and accounting
parentheses — reads as a number this file format can hold, and at
least one of prefix and suffix is non-empty. Where more than one
substring parses, the core is the LONGEST, and of equal-length
candidates the LEFTMOST: a stated total order, so the split is a
function of the cell and of nothing else, and two producers reading
one cell derive one prefix, one core and one suffix. The line is the
count `minimum_parse_rate` fixes (section 4.4), applied as a COUNT
and never as a compared share, so that no rounding of a division
decides a role; the arithmetic of the count itself is section
4.5.2's. TWO bounds are consulted, not one — the affixed-number
count against the line, the pair's cell count against the floor —
because publishing a floor-governed spelling is constitutive of this
role.

**C6-5 (the pair's identity is EXACT).** The pair is the exact text
of the trimmed cell on either side of the core — no case folding, no
inner trimming, no normalization of any kind — and there is ONE pair
per column. A column whose cells wear more than one pair past the
slack the line leaves — `$` cells among `EUR` cells, `mg` among
`MG` — does not take this role. It declines to the later rules, and
where it lands in `free_text` the competing-readings remark
(`remark_no_reading_fits`, section 4.5) says how far the affix
reading got. Mixed-affix and case-varying-affix columns are named
residual R-P4-1 rather than a partial publication: publishing a
distribution over some of a column's cells while dropping the rest
is a description of no table, and this format does not write one.

**Stragglers are permitted, up to the line and no further.** Present
cells that are not affixed numbers wearing the pair — plain numbers,
ordinary text, a cell wearing a different pair — are the ordinary
slack every parse-line rule leaves, and they are not fatal. They are
counted in the four universal cell-census counts of section 5.1 like
the cells they are, they are outside `n_affixed`, and the twin
writes them by class through the straggler constructions, one of
which — a conforming plain-number spelling that collides with no
published hole spelling and no date form — the generation method
adds for this role.

**No column an earlier rule can claim is diverted here.** Rules 9
through 11 are tested last before the fallback for exactly this
reason (section 5.2): a two-valued column whose cells share an affix
pair is `binary`, because rule 5 runs first and this rule never sees
the column; a visit column whose cells read `V1`, `V2`, `V3` stays
`categorical`, because rule 8 runs first. The reach of this role is
the columns that published nothing yesterday, and it is bounded by
the order rather than by a test of its own.

#### Stand-in numbers are judged over CORES

**C6-54.** The shipped stand-in pass is UNTOUCHED and runs where it
always ran, over whole cells, before any role rule. This role adds a
SECOND entry to that pass, at a stated later point: only after rules
1 through 8 of section 5.2 have all declined the un-removed column
does the affix split make it eligible again, and then the judging
runs over the CORES — the same outlier-and-share rule, reading
`sentinel_outlier_iqr_multiple` and `sentinel_minimum_share`, over
the core substrings instead of over the cells. The core here is
C6-4's longest-then-leftmost core, which is a function of the cell
alone and so is fixed before any pair is. Judged cells are removed,
the column is re-tallied exactly as a plain numeric column is
re-tallied today, and only THEN do rules 9 through 12 run over what
remains. So `-999 mg` inside an affixed column is read as a hole and
never averaged in.

**The order question has one answer, and the fall-through is loud.**
A column whose pair count survives removal at or above the floor and
at or above the line takes this role; a column whose pair count is
eaten below either line by removal declines to the later rules, by
the same post-removal fall-through a plain numeric column can take
today when removal thins its population. Where such a column lands
in `free_text` the competing-readings remark additionally states how
many cells the judging removed, so a column that fell because a
marker was read out of it says so in its own evidence. The verdicts
themselves are published under the landing role's publication class,
exactly as every other stand-in verdict is. Declared values keep
their whole-cell matching; nothing about declarations changes here.

#### Added keys: twenty-two

**C6-6.** Beside every universal key, a block of this role carries
twenty-two keys and no others. Seven are this role's own. Fifteen
are the quantitative set of section 6.7, computed over the CORES
rather than over the cells.

| key | JSON type | range | meaning | disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `affix_prefix` | string | possibly empty | the exact shared prefix text every counted CELL wore | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |
| `affix_suffix` | string | possibly empty, and not both empty with `affix_prefix` | the exact shared suffix text every counted CELL wore | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |
| `n_affixed` | integer ≥ 0 | at least `small_cell_floor` and at least the parse-line count, at most `n_present` (AF2, AF3) | how many present CELLS wore the pair | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |
| `n_core_numeric` | integer ≥ 0 | ≥ 1 (AF5) | CORES that read as a number this file format can hold | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |
| `n_core_out_of_range` | integer ≥ 0 | — | CORES that are well-formed numbers too large or too small for binary64 | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |
| `n_core_contradictory` | integer ≥ 0 | — | CORES written in numeric notation whose meaning conflicts with itself — a sign inside accounting parentheses | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |
| `n_core_not_numeric` | integer ≥ 0 | — | CORES that are not numeric notation at all | EXACT-OBSERVABLE |

The four core-class counts are a census of the cells `n_affixed`
counts, classified by the one shipped classifier over the core
substring alone, and they close on `n_affixed`. **For a counted cell
the core is the text between the published prefix and the published
suffix**, whether or not it parses: C6-4's total order chooses the
split on an affixed number, and the pair, once fixed, splits every
other counted cell.

**A cell can wear the pair and still not be an affixed number, and
that is what the last three core classes are for.** Only cells whose
core reads as a number this format can hold count toward C6-4's
line. A cell that wears the pair exactly and whose core is out of
range, contradictory, or no number at all is counted in `n_affixed`
and in the core class its core falls in, and sits in the slack the
line leaves. Were `n_affixed` the count of affixed numbers alone,
`n_core_out_of_range`, `n_core_contradictory` and
`n_core_not_numeric` would be zero in every conforming document by
construction, and the census would close on nothing.

**The quantitative set, key for key as section 6.7 states it, read
over the cores.** Every one is REQUIRED on this role, carries the
JSON type, range and disposition that section gives it, and answers
about the cores of the cells that wore the pair.

| key | what it describes here |
|---|---|
| `percentiles` | the eleven-rung ladder over the parsed CORE values |
| `mean`, `std`, `skew` | the same three moments, over the parsed CORE values |
| `std_unrepresentable` | true when the exact spread of the cores is larger than binary64 can hold |
| `n_zero` | parsed CORE values equal to zero |
| `n_negative` | CORES whose notation settles a negative sign |
| `n_negative_unrepresentable` | out-of-range CORES whose notation settles a negative sign |
| `n_used_in_statistics`, `n_left_out_of_statistics` | how many cores the statistics were computed from, and how many were not |
| `numeric_share` | the share of the cores whose writer meant a number |
| `integer_valued` | true when every numeric-looking CORE is a whole number — the published FACT a consumer routes the twin's integer rule on, never the role name |
| `numeric_styles` | how many CORES were written in each of the six spelling styles of section 7.5, under the floor |
| `fraction_widths` | how many `decimal`-styled CORES were written at each fraction width, under the floor; a key of the BLOCK and a sibling of `numeric_styles`, never a key inside it (C6-27) |
| `n_rows` | the table's row count, echoed; LOADER-ONLY, and Q1 confines it to `count`, `continuous` and this role |

`numeric_styles` and `fraction_widths` reach this role for the
reason they reach the other two: an `affixed_number` twin cell is
written as a parsed number from the ladder INSIDE its affix pair, so
the reader's inferred type is at stake and a style map is something
the generator can discharge.

Those twenty-two, added to the twenty-two universal keys of section
5.1, are the whole of a block of this role. Every one is present in
every such block, on every run; this format has no optional keys.

#### The two populations, kept apart

**C6-7.** A block of this role carries TWO censuses of two different
populations, and one census cannot answer for both. The four
universal counts — `n_numeric`, `n_out_of_range`, `n_contradictory`
and `n_not_numeric` — keep the meaning section 5.1 gives them and
answer for the CELLS: on a column of `u:1`, `u:2`, … no complete
cell reads as a number, so `n_numeric` is zero and `n_not_numeric`
is the present count. That is the truth about the cells and this
document does not bend it. The four core-class counts —
`n_core_numeric`, `n_core_out_of_range`, `n_core_contradictory` and
`n_core_not_numeric` — answer for the CORES, and they are the
population every quantitative key of the block describes. Every
quantitative invariant this format states over `n_numeric` is read
on this role over `n_core_numeric`, and nowhere else; every one
stated over `n_present` is read over `n_affixed`, that being the
population the cores were taken from — so `n_left_out_of_statistics`
is `n_affixed` less `n_core_numeric`, and `numeric_share`'s
denominator is `n_affixed`. The four universal counts are read over
the cells on this role exactly as on every other. A reader who takes
`n_numeric` for the ladder's population reads a zero and concludes
the block is empty, and a producer who writes the cores into
`n_numeric` writes a false statement about the cells — which is why
the two are separately named rather than shared.

The invariants of this role, its publication class, the keys
forbidden on it and the remark every column of it carries follow.
